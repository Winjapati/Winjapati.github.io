Research

Current Project: Roots of the PIE Lexicon

Question: Which sounds begin the roots of Proto-Indo-European? Is b really as rare as the handbooks say?

Data: Julius Pokorny's Indogermanisches etymologisches Wörterbuch (1959), the standard 20th-century dictionary of PIE roots, from its online edition. I cleaned it into a table of 2,077 roots with Python and pandas.

Preliminary results: I found the first sound of every root, and counted how many roots begin with each of the fifteen stops:

Place Voiceless Voiced Voiced aspirated
Labialp 133b 28bh 130
Dentalt 108d 57dh 67
Palatalk̂ 92ĝ 22ĝh 44
Velark 187g 50gh 57
Labiovelarkʷ 24gʷ 36gʷh 12

Only 28 roots begin with b, under a quarter as many as begin with p or bh. At every other place, the voiced stop has at least half as many roots as the aspirate. The gap at the lips is real.

Most of the 28 b-roots aren't ordinary words at all: they mean things like 'to bleat', 'babble', and 'sound of barking'. Sound-symbolic words often break the normal rules.
