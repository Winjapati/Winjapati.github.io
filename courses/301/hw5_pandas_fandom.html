The Assignment

Pick a show, movie, or band you love, and find the script of at least one episode or movie, or the lyrics of at least five songs by one artist, online (see Where to Find Your Text below). Copy the text into a plain text file called hw5_raw.txt in your homework/hw5 folder. Then make a Quarto document called hw5_pandas_lastname_firstname.qmd in the same folder, and start it with a note saying where you got your text. In that document, read the file into Python, clean it, and turn it into a DataFrame with one row per line: one line of dialogue, or one line of a song. Throw out anything that isn’t really a line, such as blank lines, titles, scene headings, stage directions, or labels like [Chorus]. Your table must have at least three columns taken from the text itself: the line, who says it (for a script) or which song it’s from (for lyrics), and one more of your choosing (a scene number, a line number within the song, verse vs. chorus…). Then add at least three new columns with pandas, such as the length of each line, a lowercase version of it, whether it’s a question, or whether it mentions a word you care about. Finally, save your table as hw5_lastname_firstname.csv, load it back in, and show its number of rows and its first 10 rows to prove it worked. End with a short paragraph: what does one row of your table stand for, what did you throw out and why, and what is one thing you noticed in your new columns? As always, explain in your own words what your code does, and say whether and how you used any AI tool. (The syllabus does not allow GenAI to write your homework solutions.)

Where to Find Your Text

  • TV scripts: many shows’ fan wikis on Fandom have a “Transcripts” page for each episode (search for your show’s wiki plus transcript).
  • Movie scripts: IMSDb, the Internet Movie Script Database
  • Lyrics: Genius or any other lyrics site
  • Plays: Project Gutenberg has thousands of plays, from Shakespeare to Oscar Wilde, in plain text

Stay safe: you only ever need to copy text from a web page. Never download a file, install anything, or click “Allow” on a pop-up to get your text. If a page says your computer is infected, or that you need to update something, it’s a scam: close the tab. Script and lyrics sites are full of ads, so use an ad blocker if you can (for example, uBlock Origin). If you think something got onto your computer, contact UK’s IT Service Desk.

Tip: a transcript, where each line looks like JOEY: How you doin'?, is much easier to clean than a screenplay, where the character’s name sits on its own line and the dialogue is on the lines below it. Look at a few pages before you pick! (Transcripts on Fandom wikis are often tables: when you paste one into VS Code, the speaker and the line may be separated by a tab instead of a colon. In Python, a tab is written "\t".)

Select the text in your browser, copy it, and paste it into a new file in VS Code. Save it as hw5_raw.txt.
