Questions

Two kinds of questions

  • Yes/no questions: ‘Do you want the eye?’
  • Content questions: ‘What do you want?’, ‘Where is the man?’
    • Also called wh-questions: who, what, where, when, why, how

Naq’anir: yes/no questions

  • When building the sound system and making Naq’anir tonal, it was decided that there would be a specific word to mark questions: hâ
Naq’anir English
ʂi ju kiʂ já. ‘You want the eye.’
hâ ʂi ju kiʂ já? ‘Do you want the eye?’
  • hâ always goes at the beginning, even though the verb comes last
  • But what about wh-questions?

Questions in English

  • ‘You want pizza.’
    • Make a question where pizza is replaced by what.

English usually moves its question words

  • Start with a statement: ‘You want the eye.’
  • Ask about the object: ‘You want what?’
  • English moves what to the front: ‘What do you want?’
  • This is called wh-movement

Not every language moves them

Mandarin Word for word English
Nǐ yào shénme? you want what ‘What do you want?’
Tā zài nǎr? he be.at where ‘Where is he?’
  • The question word stays in the normal position of the noun or adverb it replaces
  • This is called wh-in-situ (‘in place’)
  • Japanese does this too: Nani o tabemasu ka? ‘what OBJ eat Q’ = ‘What do you eat?’

Naq’anir: move or stay?

  • What if Naq’anir moved its question words to the front?
    • hâ ʂi já? ‘What do you want?’
    • hâ ʂi já? ‘Do you want (it)?’
  • The same sentence! hâ ‘what’ would collide with hâ the yes/no marker
  • So Naq’anir question words stay in place
    • Remember, too that Nairetha was settled by Chinese explorers!

Naq’anir: question words in place

Naq’anir Word for word English
ʂi hâ já? you what want ‘What do you want?’
ʂi hâ pápa? you what offer ‘What are you offering?’
ʂi hâ ʂaq sasa? you what about talk ‘What are you talking about?’
hu hût hâr i? the.HUM man where be ‘Where is the man?’
ʂi hâhu i? you who be ‘Who are you?’
  • All five lines come from (or near) our script!

Where did hâhu ‘who’ come from?

  • The script never needed ‘who’, so we built it from words we had:
    • hâ ‘what’ + hu ‘the (human)’ → hâhu ‘what person’ = ‘who’
  • Compare hâr ‘where’, built on ra ‘at’
  • And English wh-: what, who, where, when, why all share a piece, too
