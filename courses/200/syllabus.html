LIN 200: How to Create Your Own Language

Syllabus — Fall 2026  |  Class Index →


 Course Description

In 1910, J.R.R. Tolkien began construction of a fictional language of the Elves, which ultimately resulted in the creation of at least fifteen different languages and dialects. Why would someone create their own language? And how would a person begin to even do so? In this course, we will answer both questions directly, addressing the impetus for language invention and the process by which one creates a language. By examining a variety of invented languages (such as Esperanto, Klingon, and Dothraki) and natural languages (such as Spanish, Swahili, and Chinese), we will examine how language works in general and the typical features found within the languages of the world. Throughout the course, students will work on their own invented language in stages — recorded in a physical course journal — concluding with a final translation and performance in their invented language.

Course Learning Outcomes

Learning outcomes are a description of what a student will be able to do upon completion of the course. The statements below describe what you should reasonably expect to be able to do by the end of this course, if you participate fully in every meeting and complete your assignments and readings on time.

Upon completion of this course, students will be able to:

  • Identify the major subfields of linguistic study
  • Identify the various types of constructed languages
  • Describe and recognize the basic linguistic features found in the languages of the world
  • Apply natural linguistic laws and constraints — or deliberately violate them — in the design of an original language
  • Critically analyze work produced by other students, incorporating an understanding of the history, theory, and cultural factors relevant to invented languages
  • Evaluate, reassess, and refine their own language creation over the course of the semester
  • Produce a final translation and performance that demonstrates concepts learned throughout the semester

Skills You'll Build

Beyond linguistics content, this course is designed to build several of the 10 essential skills in the Kentucky Graduate Profile (see UK's Wildcat Workforce initiative), which outlines the skills employers most want to see in Kentucky postsecondary graduates. In this class, you'll practice:

  • Communication: you'll record a final performance/translation in your invented language and present your work to the class.
  • Critical and Creative Thinking: you'll design an original language from the ground up, applying (or deliberately breaking) real linguistic constraints.
  • Knowledge Application: you'll apply concepts from descriptive and theoretical linguistics to a sustained creative project.
  • Information Literacy: you'll research natural and invented languages to inform choices in your own language's design.
  • Collaboration and Teamwork: you'll workshop your language in progress with classmates and give/receive feedback throughout the semester.

Course Journal

The primary project of this course is the creation of your own invented language, recorded in stages in a physical journal. The journal is organized into four sequential units, each concluding with an in-class presentation and graded as a checkpoint, followed by a final translation and performance:

1. Creating Your Culture: What is the name and concept behind your invented language? What culture is it based in or does it belong to?
2. Creating Your Sound System: What are the sounds of your language, and how are they organized (consonants, vowels, phonotactics, prosody)?
3. Creating Your Word System: How are words built and arranged in your language (lexical categories, nominals, verbs, word order)?
4. Creating Your History & Writing System: How has your language changed over time, and how is it written down — what script or notation represents its sounds and words?
5. Final Project: Using everything developed in your journal, translate and perform a passage from your English-language composition (see below) in your invented language for the class.

English-Language Composition

Alongside your journal, you'll draft an original piece of media in English — a short story, poem, song, or other form — that gives insight into your invented language's culture. This is the piece you'll eventually translate (in part) for your Final Project, so it needs to be substantially drafted early: by the end of the Sound System stage, so that you have a concrete sense of the vocabulary and concepts you'll need to build out when you move into the Word System stage.

Journal: Composition Outline (due 9/18): A brief outline or opening fragment — premise, characters or speakers, setting, and how it reflects the culture you're building.
Journal: Composition (due 9/25): A substantially complete English-language draft of the full piece. From this point on, you'll be drawing directly on it to identify the words and concepts your invented language needs.

Your composition continues to be refined through the Word System and History & Writing System stages as your language's vocabulary and writing system take shape, culminating in a translated passage performed for the Final Project.

AI Policy

All work submitted for this course — your course journal, composition, and any other graded materials — must be 100% your own original work. Generative AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, or similar) may not be used at any stage of your journal or composition work, including brainstorming, drafting, translation, grammar/writing polish, or fact-checking. The purpose of this course is for you to build your own invented language and composition from the ground up; using GenAI to generate or substantially shape that work undermines the core learning goal of the course and will be treated as a violation of academic integrity.
