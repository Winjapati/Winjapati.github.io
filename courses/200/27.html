Heads

What is a head?

  • Every phrase is built around one word: its head
    • ‘the quick fox in the forest’ is about a fox: a noun phrase
    • ‘sees the man from the grass’ is about seeing: a verb phrase
    • ‘in the forest’ is built on in: a prepositional phrase
  • Word order is mostly about where the head goes

Three places for a head

Type Where the head goes
Head-initial first: head + everything else
Head-final last: everything else + head
Transitional in the middle
  • Languages tend to put their heads in the same place in every kind of phrase

Noun Phrases

Noun Phrases

  • Consists of:
    • Nouns (N): this is the head
    • Determiners (a(n), the, this, my; Det)
    • Adjectives (A)
    • Prepositional Phrases (PP)
  • There is a set word order in English

English: Det + A + N + PP

  • The quick fox in the forest
  • Det + A + N + PP

Every English NP works this way

  • That old chair on the floor
  • Det + A + N + PP

Possible Word Orders in NPs

  • The fox quick in the forest (Spanish): Det + N + A + PP
  • The quick in the forest fox (Japanese): Det + A + PP + N
  • Fox quick the in the forest (Welsh): N + A + Det + PP

Naq’anir: which do we choose?

  • ‘that good man in the boardroom’
  • The pieces:
ku líri hût ju p’irq’aʂ ul
‘that’ ‘being good’ ‘man’ ‘the boardroom in’
Det A N PP
  • Yes, ul ‘in’ comes after ju p’irq’aʂ. More on that soon!

Naq’anir: three options

Order Naq’anir Like
Det + N + A + PP ku hût líri ju p’irq’aʂ ul Spanish
Det + A + PP + N ku líri ju p’irq’aʂ ul hût Japanese
N + A + Det + PP hût líri ku ju p’irq’aʂ ul Welsh
  • Note where the head (hût) is in each sentence

Where is the head?

Order Head Type
Det + N + A + PP in the middle Transitional
Det + A + PP + N last Head-final
N + A + Det + PP first Head-initial
  • English (Det + A + N + PP) has the head in the middle, too

How to choose?

  1. Languages with suffixes tend to be head-final
  2. Languages with prefixes tend to be head-initial
  3. Languages with neither (or a mix of the two) tend to be head-initial or transitional
  • Look at your affixes from the last two weeks! Did you pick suffixes, prefixes, a mix of both?

Naq’anir’s affixes

Affix Type Example
-ĩ, -tĩ suffix hût → hûtĩ ‘men’
-i, -ri suffix lí → líri ‘being good’
-mâ ‘not’ suffix kiʂ → kiʂmâ ‘blind’
-pa suffix θi ‘die’ → θipa ‘kill’
-tâ suffix sasa → sasatâ ‘speaks (HON)’
q’á- prefix kíʂ → q’ákíʂ ‘be seen’
ul- ‘in’ prefix lu ‘go’ → ullu ‘enter’
  • Mostly suffixes (only two prefixes) → mostly head-final

Naq’anir: the noun phrase

  • Day 22: ‘the’ comes before the noun: hu hût ‘the man’
  • Day 23: the participle comes before the noun: líri hût ‘good man’
  • So the modifiers already come first: Det + A + N
  • But with some prefixes, we can posit transitional order here, placing PP after the noun:
Naq’anir Order
ku líri hût ju p’irq’aʂ ul Det + A + N + PP
‘that good man in the boardroom’

Verb Phrases

Verb Phrases

  • Consists of:
    • Verbs (V): this is the head
    • Noun Phrases (NP)
    • Prepositional Phrases (PP)
  • There is a set word order in English

English: V + NP + PP

  • Eyes the man from the grass.
  • V + NP + PP

Naq’anir: which do we choose?

  • ‘sees the robot from the church’
  • The pieces:
kíʂ ʈʂa ʈʂáʈʂ tâ tâlq’aʂ lǎ
‘sees’ ‘the (machine) robot’ ‘the (divine) church from’
V NP PP

Naq’anir: two options

Order Naq’anir Type
V + NP + PP kíʂ ʈʂa ʈʂáʈʂ tâ tâlq’aʂ lǎ Head-initial
NP + PP + V ʈʂa ʈʂáʈʂ tâ tâlq’aʂ lǎ kíʂ Head-final
  • Which one fits a mostly suffixing language?
    • Head-final or head-initial?

Naq’anir: NP + PP + V

  • Mostly suffixes → head-final: the verb comes last
  • The VP is the mirror image of English:
    • ‘sees the robot from the church’ → ‘the robot from the church sees’
  • lu (future) and qup (completed) stay right before the verb
Naq’anir English
ʈʂa ʈʂáʈʂ tâ tâlq’aʂ lǎ kíʂ ‘sees the robot from the church’
ʈʂa ʈʂáʈʂ tâ tâlq’aʂ lǎ lu kíʂ ‘will see the robot from the church’
pût’ ju lap’ ʐa ra t’ip ‘sits at the end of the table’

Prepositional Phrases

Prepositional Phrases

  • Consists of:
    • Prepositions (P): this is the head
    • Noun Phrase (NP)
  • There is a set word order in English

Prepositional vs. Postpositional

Head-initial: prepositions Head-final: postpositions
‘in bed’ *‘bed in’
‘on the way’ *‘the way on’
‘from my nose’ *‘my nose from’
‘with a spoon’ *‘a spoon with’
*‘ago 20 years’ ‘20 years ago’
  • English is mostly prepositional, with one postposition: ago

Naq’anir: a change of plans

  • On Day 22 we wrote ul q’aʂ ‘in a building’, with ul first
  • But Naq’anir is mostly head-final, so ul should come last
  • Same words, new position: they’re postpositions now
    • This is normal in conlanging! Later decisions often change earlier ones. You can go back and revise.

Naq’anir: postpositions

Meaning Naq’anir English
Location ju p’irq’aʂ ul ‘in the boardroom’
Source tâ tâlq’aʂ lǎ ‘from the church’
Instrument ʐíqi kiʂ wa ‘with a new eye’
Direction ʂi ti ‘to you’
Possession málʈʂiq ʐa ‘of Malchekk’
  • NP + P: head-final, like the VP

Sentences

Sentences

  • The 3 most common word orders:
Order Example Type
VSO ‘Kicks the boy the ball.’ Head-initial
SOV ‘The boy the ball kicks.’ Head-final
SVO ‘The boy kicks the ball.’ Transitional
  • What do all three of these have in common?
    • Why do you think that is?

Naq’anir: S O V

  • Head-final, so the verb comes last: S O V
    • Day 24’s order (S V O) changes!
Naq’anir English
málʈʂiq pût’ ju lap’ ʐa ra t’ip. ‘Malchekk sits at the end of the table.’
hṹ ʂâ ju kiʂ ju lap’ át kíʂ. ‘Houn Sha sees the eye on the table.’
tâl kĩ θâq’ĩ na ti pa. ‘The Lord gives me these powers.’

S O V: the gloss

S O PP V
tâl kĩ θâq’ĩ na ti pa
Lord these power.PL I to give
na ʂi kíʂ
I you see
  • The person you give to goes in a PP with ti ‘to’, after the object
  • na ʂi kíʂ ‘I see you’; ʂi na kíʂ ‘You see me’
