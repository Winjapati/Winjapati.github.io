<h2>Journal: Composition Translation</h2>
<p><em>Module: Final Project &middot; 20 pts &middot; Due Monday 11/23 by 9:59am</em></p>

<p>You've translated your first 50 words. Now translate the <strong>entire</strong> English-language composition into your conlang, using the four-step process from Days 29&ndash;31:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Simplify</strong> your composition</li>
<li><strong>Convert</strong> the word order from English to your conlang's</li>
<li><strong>Mark</strong> the grammar your language requires</li>
<li><strong>Replace</strong> English words and affixes with your conlang's</li>
</ol>
<p>This translation is the foundation for your Final Project: the passage you perform comes from it, and the interlinear you write in your journal for the Final Product is built from it. Use the <a href="#">Naq'anir model translation</a> as your guide to format.</p>

<h3>What to submit</h3>
<p>One typed document (PDF or .docx) with three parts:</p>

<p><strong>Part 1: Line-by-line translation.</strong> Go through your composition one sentence at a time. For each sentence, give:</p>
<ol type="a">
<li>The <strong>original</strong> English sentence</li>
<li>Your <strong>simplified</strong> English version (if you changed it)</li>
<li>The sentence <strong>in your conlang</strong></li>
<li>A <strong>word-by-word gloss</strong> lined up under the conlang line, with grammatical markers labeled (e.g., <code>eye-PL</code>, <code>FUT</code>, <code>NEG</code>, <code>be.right-CAUS</code>)</li>
<li>A <strong>free translation</strong> in quotation marks</li>
</ol>
<p>Example (Naq'anir):</p>
<pre>
Original:   Your eyes are failing you.
Simplified: Your eyes fail.

kiʂ-ĩ   ʂi   ʐa  t'ûq'.
eye-PL  you  of  fail
'Your eyes are failing.'
</pre>

<p><strong>Part 2: The full translation.</strong> Your entire composition in your conlang as continuous text, with no glosses, the way a speaker of your language would read it. (Romanization is fine here; you don't need to use your writing system for this checkpoint.)</p>

<p><strong>Part 3: New decisions.</strong> Translating the whole piece will expose gaps in your grammar and lexicon. List them in two short sections:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Grammar gaps:</strong> each structure you didn't have (negation, &ldquo;must,&rdquo; comparatives, relative clauses, etc.), and what you did about it &mdash; either <em>simplified it away</em> (Step 1) or <em>made a decision</em> and added it to your grammar. Give one example sentence for each.</li>
<li><strong>New words:</strong> every word you added to your lexicon while translating, with its meaning.</li>
</ul>
<p>Then update your journal's Word System section to match. Consistency between your grammar, your lexicon, and your translation is part of what's graded in the Final Product.</p>

<h3>Tips</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Simplify ruthlessly.</strong> If a sentence needs grammar you don't have, rewrite the English before you build new grammar.</li>
<li><strong>Watch for English leaking through:</strong> possessives, prepositional phrases, adjective placement, question words, and words your conlang doesn't have (<em>a</em>, <em>the</em>, <em>is</em>, <em>do</em>, <em>will</em>, <em>-ing</em>&hellip;).</li>
<li><strong>Be consistent.</strong> The same English word or ending should come out the same way every time unless your grammar says otherwise.</li>
</ul>

<h3>AI policy reminder</h3>
<p>Per the syllabus, generative AI may not be used at any stage of this work, including translation, glossing, or checking. The translation must be entirely your own.</p>

<p><strong>Submission:</strong> Upload your document to this Canvas assignment by <strong>9:59am on Monday 11/23</strong>. Standard late policy applies (2% per day, up to 20%).</p>

## Rubric (20 pts)

| Criterion | Full | Partial | Minimal / None |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Completeness** (4) | 4: Entire composition translated, every sentence | 2–3: Most translated; some sentences or a section missing | 0–1: Only a fragment (e.g., little beyond the 50-word quiz) |
| **Simplification** (2) | 2: Simplified English given where needed; simplifications keep the meaning and avoid structures the grammar lacks | 1: Simplification inconsistent, or complex English translated without the grammar to support it | 0: No simplification shown |
| **Word order** (4) | 4: Consistently follows the conlang's stated order (sentence, NP, VP, PP, questions) | 2–3: Mostly correct; English order slips through in places (possessives, PPs, adjectives) | 0–1: Largely English word order |
| **Grammatical marking** (4) | 4: Every category the grammar requires is marked, correctly and consistently (number, case, tense/aspect, mood, voice, etc.) | 2–3: Most marked; some categories missing or inconsistent | 0–1: Little marking; English function words left in |
| **Lexicon & consistency** (2) | 2: All words from the lexicon; same word or affix rendered the same way throughout; no leftover English | 1: Occasional inconsistencies or untranslated English words | 0: Frequent inconsistencies |
| **Interlinear format** (2) | 2: Every sentence has conlang line, aligned gloss with labeled morphemes, and free translation; full continuous text included (Part 2) | 1: Glosses incomplete, misaligned, or Part 2 missing | 0: No glossing |
| **New decisions documented** (2) | 2: Grammar gaps and new words listed with examples; consistent with the translation | 1: List incomplete or missing examples | 0: No list |
